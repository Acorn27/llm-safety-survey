Use in 2025-2026
- VERIFIED (primary), Meta "Muse Spark Safety & Preparedness Report", arXiv 2606.12429.
  - AgentHarm is the direct-misuse robustness eval (Sec. 1.1.2 and 3.2.1, Table 2). The report uses the test_public split, per the search summary, which I did not independently confirm in the report body.
  - Compliance rate on harmful tasks: Muse Spark 26.0%, GPT-5.4 10.2%, Claude Opus 4.6 8.2%, Gemini 3.1 Pro 21.6%.
  - False refusals on the benign subset: 6.8%, 16.5%, 20.9% and 6.9% respectively.
  - Source read: https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12429
  - Caveat: the document's date is inconsistent. The text says 24 Aug 2026, the arXiv page lists v1 on 14 May 2026, the ID implies June 2026, and the PDF metadata says 2026-06-12. Cite as "Meta, 2026".
- VERIFIED (primary), International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science, joint agentic testing exercise, arXiv 2601.15679 (22 Jan 2026, 70 authors from Singapore, Japan, Australia, Canada, EC, France, Kenya, South Korea and the UK).
  - A table of adopted datasets includes AgentHarm. It lists sensitive-information-leakage (4 tasks) and fraud (28 tasks) tasks "extracted from the AgentHarm dataset". The paper also adopts AgentDojo tasks.
  - The paper is methodological and anonymizes models ("Model E" and so on).
- VERIFIED, UK AISI Inspect Evals ships it. The paper was a UK AISI collaboration.
- NOT FOUND in these frontier-lab documents (full-text search of PDFs; zero hits): Anthropic Claude Opus 4.6, Sonnet 5, Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5 system cards; OpenAI GPT-5.6 system card. The one frontier-company document found that reports it is Meta's.
  - Not checked in full: GPT-6 Astra PDF (its prompt-injection page names no public benchmark other than Gray Swan IPI), Gemini 3.x PDFs. The Gemini 3.7 Flash model card page names no external benchmarks.
- A FutureAGI glossary claim that "every frontier-lab model card reports AgentHarm" is UNVERIFIED and contradicted by the above.

Criticisms and limitations
- VERIFIED (Inspect page): partial public split, scoring-version changes (v6-B recommended, comparability across versions not guaranteed), "will not be necessarily suitable for all evaluation contexts", and grades should not be read as an overall measure of agent safety.
- VERIFIED (arXiv 2607.28685, 30 Jul 2026, Wang et al.), "Safety, or Just Capability? A Validity Audit of Agent-Safety Benchmarks".
  - It audits R-Judge, InjecAgent, AgentHarm and AgentDojo on up to 22 models. It finds the benchmarks rank the same models inconsistently.
  - AgentHarm shows the strongest held-out association (rho = +0.72) with three-template jailbreak safety after controlling for capability. The authors read that as convergent validity between two harmful-compliance measures, "rather than general safety".
  - Capability correlates negatively with misalignment safety (rho = -0.44, n = 21). Small panels can flip trade-off signs.
- Secondary (UNVERIFIED in primary text): OS-Harm (arXiv 2506.14866) is reported to criticize AgentHarm's hand-written scoring functions as unable to anticipate all scenarios, and to warn that failure may reflect low capability rather than alignment.
- The judge is a GPT-4o refusal/acceptance classifier plus hand-written grader functions (reported by a validity paper summary; not checked in the AgentHarm PDF).
- Saturation: no evidence found (Meta's 26% compliance leaves headroom).

Security-venue and blog angle
- No USENIX Security, S&P, CCS or NDSS paper introduced it. ICLR 2025 only.
- Gray Swan blog: https://www.grayswan.ai/blog/agentharm (appeared in search results; content not read).
- UK AISI publication page https://www.aisi.gov.uk/publications/agentharm-a-benchmark-for-measuring-harmfulness-of-ai-agents appeared in search results; content not read.

Judge reliability numbers (the earlier note's 16.4% / 37.9%)
- VERIFIED in the primary paper, Sec. 8.5: the "selected ensemble" evaluator has a 16.4% false non-violating rate (hazardous responses judged safe) and a 37.9% false violating rate (safe responses flagged). The paper says both "remain above the benchmark's target", and gives no numeric target.
- Caveats found in the paper:
  - Sec. 8.5 does not state the dataset the rates were computed on. The ground-truth set is 859 human-annotated responses from 15 seeds, 10 attacks and 12 SUTs. Only 40 were multiply annotated (inter-rater reliability 0.88 on the final judgment; 0.54 and 0.63 on sub-judgments). The rest were annotated by one reviewer.
  - The ensemble was tuned to keep false negatives low, which raises false positives.
  - The authors say absolute unsafe rates "should not be treated as precise point estimates", and that the evaluator supports relative grading only.
  - Per-system and per-hazard results are withheld because of the evaluator's error and only 24 seeds per hazard. Qwen 2.5 7B was used to generate one attack and is also a system under test, so its gap may be biased upward. Two planned systems were dropped after provider changes. Closed API systems are deferred.
  - Jailbreak 1.1 is planned with a larger adjudicated ground-truth set and a new evaluator architecture.
- The French article (https://www.actuia.com/actualite/jailbreak-mlcommons-publie-ses-resultats-et-les-erreurs-de-son-juge/, dated 5 Oct 2026) reports the same numbers and itself notes the dataset is unspecified. It is consistent with the paper.

Adoption: none found. No press release, lab system card or government use of the Jailbreak benchmark found as of 2026-10-13. The v0.5 announcement names AI Verify Foundation and IMDA as partners.

### Gray Swan IPI Arena paper (strongest 2026 candidate surfaced)
- Title: "How Vulnerable Are AI Agents to Indirect Prompt Injections? Insights from a Large-Scale Public Competition", arXiv 2603.15714, submitted 16 Mar 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15714.
- Authors: Mateusz Dziemian and 30 co-authors (including Matt Fredrikson and Zico Kolter). The full list was not retrieved: UNVERIFIED beyond that.
- Content: 464 participants, 272,000 attack attempts, 13 frontier models, 41 scenarios (tool use, coding, computer use), 8,648 successful attacks. Every model was vulnerable. ASR ranged from 0.5% (Claude Opus 4.5) to 8.5% (Gemini 2.5 Pro). Capability and robustness were weakly correlated. Quarterly competitions are planned to counter saturation. Partial data is on HF and code on GitHub (github.com/grayswansecurity/ipi_arena_os, from a search result). The full dataset went to UK AISI and CAISI.
- Government: described in NIST's CAISI blog of 23 Mar 2026 (credits CAISI, Gray Swan, UK AISI and labs).
- Predecessor: the ART benchmark, "Security Challenges in AI Agent Deployment: Insights from a Large Scale Public Competition", arXiv 2507.20526 (28 Jul 2025; Andy Zou, Maxwell Lin, Eliot Jones and 14 others), developed with UK AISI. It is cited in the Opus 4.6 card, then deprecated in Sonnet 5.
- This is the 2026 lead that "genuinely has influence already": it is cited by Anthropic (Opus 5, Sonnet 5, 5.5), OpenAI (GPT-6 Astra) and Google (Gemini 4 Argon blog), and by CAISI. It is a natural addition or substitute for the survey's prompt-injection slot. Citation count not retrieved: UNVERIFIED. (It is a prompt-injection benchmark, not a general harmful-content one.)
