# Verification of 2024 finalists (CSE 6324 LLM safety survey)

Checked 2026-10-13. Legend: VERIFIED = confirmed on the cited primary page, with URL. VERIFIED (snippet) = confirmed only through a search-result excerpt of the primary document, because the PDF was not machine-readable in this session; re-open before quoting numbers. UNVERIFIED = not confirmed; reason given.

Citation counts: Semantic Scholar Graph API (batch endpoint, arXiv IDs), queried 2026-10-13. Google Scholar was not queried. The S2 API was rate-limited, so the counts were fetched on retry. Source for all six: https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/batch (fields=citationCount).

Method limits: Anthropic, xAI and some OpenAI system-card PDFs were compressed PDFs that the fetch tool could not decode, and no PDF-to-text tool was installed. Claims about those are tagged "(snippet)" or UNVERIFIED. The Gemini and Meta cards were not found to cite any of these benchmarks. I could not exhaustively search every card.

## 1. HarmBench

- Exact title: VERIFIED. "HarmBench: A Standardized Evaluation Framework for Automated Red Teaming and Robust Refusal". https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249
- Authors (12, in order): VERIFIED. Mantas Mazeika, Long Phan, Xuwang Yin, Andy Zou, Zifan Wang, Norman Mu, Elham Sakhaee, Nathaniel Li, Steven Basart, Bo Li, David Forsyth, Dan Hendrycks. (Same list on https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mazeika24a.html)
- Venue: VERIFIED. ICML 2024, PMLR vol. 235, pp. 35181-35224. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mazeika24a.html. The arXiv page itself lists no venue (only harmbench.org). arXiv v1 6 Feb 2024, v2 27 Feb 2024.
- PDF: VERIFIED. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.04249 and https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/mazeika24a/mazeika24a.pdf
- Code and data: VERIFIED. https://github.com/centerforaisafety/HarmBench (MIT license; classifiers cais/HarmBench-Llama-2-13b-cls, ...-multimodal-behaviors, and cais/HarmBench-Mistral-7b-val-cls; HarmBench 1.0 released 2024/02/26). Project site: harmbench.org (named on arXiv). No standalone HarmBench leaderboard found.
- Size and what it measures: VERIFIED (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04249). 510 unique harmful behaviors = 400 textual (200 standard, 100 contextual, 100 copyright) + 110 multimodal. 7 semantic categories (cybercrime and intrusion; chem/bio weapons and drugs; copyright; misinformation; harassment; illegal activities; general harm). It compares 18 red-teaming attack methods across 33 target LLMs and defenses, and measures attack success rate (ASR) via a fine-tuned classifier. Classifier agreement with human labels (Table 3): 93.19 avg vs GPT-4 88.37.
- Citations: 1,676 (S2, 2026-10-13).
- Use in 2025-2026:
  - HELM Safety v1.0 (Stanford CRFM, Nov 2024) includes HarmBench as one of five scenarios, with BBQ, SimpleSafetyTests, AnthropicRedTeam and XSTest. VERIFIED. https://crfm.stanford.edu/2024/11/08/helm-safety.html. The HELM safety release v1.7.0 (May 2025) exists per a search listing (https://nlp.stanford.edu/helm/safety/benchmark_output/releases/v1.7.0/groups/). I could not render the live leaderboard (JS page).
  - Future of Life Institute AI Safety Index, Summer 2025: uses "HarmBench (jailbreak resistance)" via HELM Safety, and Cisco's 50 HarmBench prompts. VERIFIED. https://futureoflife.org/ai-safety-index-summer-2025/
  - Cisco and UPenn red-team of DeepSeek R1 (early 2025): automated jailbreak on 50 uniformly sampled HarmBench prompts, 100% ASR reported. VERIFIED (snippet, via secondary write-ups; the Cisco blog is https://blogs.cisco.com/security/evaluating-security-risk-in-deepseek-and-other-frontier-reasoning-models, not opened). Small-sample caveat.
  - Frontier lab system cards (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI): NOT FOUND citing HarmBench. UNVERIFIED that any do.
  - UK AISI / US CAISI: NOT FOUND naming HarmBench. The CAISI DeepSeek evaluation (https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/2025/09/30/CAISI_Evaluation_of_DeepSeek_AI_Models.pdf) reports private and public benchmarks, but no search result names HarmBench. UNVERIFIED.
  - Inspect Evals: not in the listing I could read (https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/inspect_evals/). The GitHub directory view was truncated, so absence is not proven.
  - USENIX Security 2025 paper TwinBreak evaluates on AdvBench, JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT and HarmBench (VERIFIED (snippet); https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity25-krauss.pdf). This shows academic reuse only.
- Criticisms and limitations:
  - Judge: a June 2026 single-author preprint finds the HarmBench classifier over-flags on 596 human-labeled completions (precision 0.835, recall 0.974). VERIFIED (abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25487. Preprint, not peer reviewed.
  - A separate paper reported a 26.8% false-positive rate and 78.3% agreement for the HarmBench classifier on other data (VERIFIED (snippet), not opened; from the search listing, unidentified paper). Do not cite until opened.
  - Construct and saturation: HarmBench has no stated freshness mechanism. Anthropic and OpenAI say their own single-turn jailbreak/harm sets are saturating (see StrongREJECT section), which is indirect evidence only.
  - JailbreakBench is partly built from HarmBench and AdvBench behaviors, so overlap and contamination between the two are plausible (inference from the JBB paper's source description; not measured).
- Blog angle: CAIS project site harmbench.org exists. No Carlini or Weng post specifically about HarmBench was found. UNVERIFIED.

## 2. JailbreakBench

- Exact title: VERIFIED. "JailbreakBench: An Open Robustness Benchmark for Jailbreaking Large Language Models". https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01318
- Authors (12, in order): VERIFIED. Patrick Chao, Edoardo Debenedetti, Alexander Robey, Maksym Andriushchenko, Francesco Croce, Vikash Sehwag, Edgar Dobriban, Nicolas Flammarion, George J. Pappas, Florian Tramer, Hamed Hassani, Eric Wong.
- Venue: VERIFIED. NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks Track (arXiv v5 comment: "camera-ready version of JailbreakBench v1.0 (accepted at NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks Track)"). Proceedings PDF: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/63092d79154adebd7305dfd498cbff70-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf. arXiv v1 28 Mar 2024, v5 31 Oct 2024. Primary category cs.CR.
- PDF: VERIFIED. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.01318
- Code, data, leaderboard: VERIFIED (https://github.com/JailbreakBench/jailbreakbench). Leaderboard https://jailbreakbench.github.io/. Dataset https://huggingface.co/datasets/JailbreakBench/JBB-Behaviors. Code is MIT. Dataset license not stated on the GitHub page; UNVERIFIED.
- Size and what it measures: VERIFIED. JBB-Behaviors has 200 behaviors: 100 harmful (10 categories aligned with OpenAI usage policies; mix of original items and items from AdvBench and HarmBench) plus 100 matched benign behaviors for over-refusal. It also provides a repository of jailbreak artifacts, a standardized threat model, system prompts, chat templates and scoring functions, and a leaderboard of attacks and defenses. v5 adds a Llama-3-70B jailbreak judge, a 300-example human-preference set for judge selection, and a Llama-3-8B refusal judge.
- Citations: 704 (S2, 2026-10-13).
- Use in 2025-2026:
  - Official leaderboard (jailbreakbench.github.io). Listing seen in search; live contents not read.
  - Academic reuse: TwinBreak, USENIX Security 2025 (VERIFIED (snippet)). A 2026 safety-tuning report (JT-SAFE-V2, arXiv 2605.24414) reports a JailbreakBench score of 99.30 (VERIFIED (snippet)). Neither is a frontier lab.
  - Frontier system cards (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, xAI), UK AISI/CAISI, HELM, Inspect Evals (not on the listing I read), Epoch, Artificial Analysis: NOT FOUND. UNVERIFIED that any use it.
- Criticisms and limitations:
  - The authors state that some behaviors are borderline and providers may disagree on whether to refuse; that ASRs on original behaviors are lower, likely due to category imbalance; and that attacker scope was restricted. VERIFIED (snippet of the paper).
  - Only 100 harmful behaviors; coverage is limited. A fixed public set raises contamination risk (inference; no measurement found).
  - Judge reliability: 2026 work argues jailbreak judges, including Llama-based ones, are miscalibrated (e.g. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25487, which measured HarmBench's classifier, not JBB's).
  - A 2026 review notes JBB is a hybrid of original entries and AdvBench/HarmBench items, so cross-paper comparisons need care (VERIFIED (snippet), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.20351).

Two purposes: evaluating hazardous knowledge, and benchmarking unlearning (the paper also introduces the RMU unlearning method). It is a knowledge proxy, not an uplift or capability-in-the-wild test.

The Gemini card itself was not opened; UNVERIFIED directly. The Gemini 3.1 Pro model card page (https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-3-1-pro/) does not name WMDP (VERIFIED absent on that page).
  - Inspect Evals (UK AISI-linked) includes WMDP (VERIFIED above).
  - Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta cards: no WMDP found. UNVERIFIED.
  - UK AISI/CAISI reports naming WMDP: NOT FOUND.
- Criticisms and limitations:
  - Authors: WMDP is a proxy; a high score does not mean a model is unsafe; RMU may "obfuscate knowledge more than unlearn it"; relearning after fine-tuning is out of scope; RMU degrades nearby topics (virology, computer security). VERIFIED (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.03218; first 100k characters read).
  - Epoch AI (Jun 2025): WMDP "only tests generally hazardous knowledge in textbook-like fashion", is not optimized for practical lab knowledge, and, with two other benchmarks, is "now largely saturated" (VCT being the exception). VERIFIED.
  - Models can be induced to sandbag on WMDP (VERIFIED (snippet), secondary summary; unnamed paper).
- Blog angle: CAIS and Anthropic blogs were not checked. UNVERIFIED.

## 4. StrongREJECT

- Exact title: VERIFIED. "A StrongREJECT for Empty Jailbreaks". https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10260
- Authors (11, in order): VERIFIED. Alexandra Souly, Qingyuan Lu, Dillon Bowen, Tu Trinh, Elvis Hsieh, Sana Pandey, Pieter Abbeel, Justin Svegliato, Scott Emmons, Olivia Watkins, Sam Toyer.
- Venue: NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks Track is REAL. VERIFIED. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e2e06adf560b0706d3b1ddfca9f29756-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.html (the proceedings page states the track). The arXiv page lists no venue. v1 15 Feb 2024, v2 27 Aug 2024. The earlier note's "(unverified)" is now resolved.
- PDF: VERIFIED. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10260 ; https://neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/e2e06adf560b0706d3b1ddfca9f29756-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf
- Code, data, docs: VERIFIED. https://github.com/dsbowen/strong_reject (MIT code; dataset aggregates prompts from prior sets under their own licenses) and https://strong-reject.readthedocs.io/. Two evaluators: rubric-based (LLM) and a fine-tuned Gemma-2B (gated).
- Size and what it measures: dataset of 313 prompts (VERIFIED (snippet) of a secondary search summary; not confirmed on a primary page; Inspect Evals lists 324 samples, a discrepancy I could not resolve, likely a different version or processing). Six categories: disinformation/deception; illegal goods and services; hate/harassment/discrimination; non-violent crimes; violence; sexual content (VERIFIED, GitHub README). It measures jailbreak effectiveness. The autograder scores both willingness to respond and specificity/convincingness of the harmful content, to avoid "empty jailbreaks". Authors report that existing evaluators significantly overstate jailbreak success and that jailbreaks bypassing safety fine-tuning tend to reduce capability (VERIFIED, abstract).
- Citations: 433 (S2, 2026-10-13).
- Use in 2025-2026 (strongest of the four):
  - OpenAI o1 system card (Dec 2024): StrongREJECT among four jailbreak evals, reported with goodness@0.1. VERIFIED (snippet). https://cdn.openai.com/o1-system-card-20241205.pdf ; arXiv copy https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16720.
  - OpenAI GPT-5 family: StrongReject tables in GPT-5-Codex addendum (15 Sep 2025; https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/97cc5669-7a25-4e63-b15f-5fd5bdc4d149/gpt-5-codex-system-card.pdf) and GPT-5 Instant "sensitive conversations" addendum (27 Oct 2025). VERIFIED (snippet); PDFs not decodable here. Reported values (0.992-0.997 for gpt-5-codex) come from search summaries only.
  - GPT-5.2 (11 Dec 2025): "an adaptation of the academic jailbreak eval, StrongReject" with the original set filtered to remove saturated examples; gpt-5.2-instant scored lower than gpt-5.1-instant, partly attributed to grader issues. VERIFIED. https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-2 (table values not in the extracted text).
  - GPT-5.5 system card: "Ahead of the GPT-5.4 launch, we replaced our previous StrongReject-based benchmark with a more challenging multiturn jailbreak evaluation". VERIFIED. https://deploymentsafety.openai.com/gpt-5-5. This is key evidence of saturation and retirement in a frontier-lab context.
  - Anthropic Claude Opus 4 / Sonnet 4 system card (May 2025) has a StrongREJECT section: run without ASL-3 protections, attacker-generated jailbreaks. VERIFIED (snippet) via search excerpts; PDF https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/6d8a8055020700718b0c49369f60816ba2a7c285/Claude%204%20System%20Card.pdf was not decodable. Secondary sources conflict on numbers (31.95% to 18.21% to 2.24% in one; 2.24% vs 10.22% in another), so do not quote figures until the PDF is read. Anthropic's Sonnet 4.5 card is said to call single-turn violative/benign sets saturated (VERIFIED (snippet), https://www.anthropic.com/claude-sonnet-4-5-system-card, not opened).
  - Inspect Evals: StrongREJECT listed as a safeguards eval, 324 samples. VERIFIED. https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/inspect_evals/
  - Third party: "A Safety Report on GPT-5.2, Gemini 3 Pro, Qwen3-VL, Doubao 1.8, Grok 4.1 Fast..." includes StrongREJECT (VERIFIED (snippet), https://arxiv.org/html/2601.10527v1). StrongREJECT also appears in the USENIX Security 2025 TwinBreak paper.
  - Google DeepMind, Meta, xAI cards; UK AISI / CAISI: not found citing StrongREJECT. UNVERIFIED.
- Criticisms and limitations:
  - Saturation: OpenAI filtered the set for saturated examples (GPT-5.2) and then replaced it (by GPT-5.4). VERIFIED.
  - Judge/grader: OpenAI cited grader issues in the GPT-5.2 results. VERIFIED. The 2026 judge-calibration preprint compares judges but does not isolate StrongREJECT.
  - Contamination/data: the prompt set is assembled from earlier public datasets (AdvBench, DAN, etc.), so it is not novel and is public (VERIFIED, README license note).
  - Single-turn only. A multiturn replacement at OpenAI implies this limitation. (Inference.)
- Blog angle: BAIR blog post on StrongREJECT exists by the authors' group but was not opened. UNVERIFIED.

## 5. SORRY-Bench (near-miss)

- Exact title: VERIFIED. "SORRY-Bench: Systematically Evaluating Large Language Model Safety Refusal" (arXiv listing; the S2 title adds "Behavior"). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14598
- Authors (16, in order): VERIFIED. Tinghao Xie, Xiangyu Qi, Yi Zeng, Yangsibo Huang, Udari Madhushani Sehwag, Kaixuan Huang, Luxi He, Boyi Wei, Dacheng Li, Ying Sheng, Ruoxi Jia, Bo Li, Kai Li, Danqi Chen, Peter Henderson, Prateek Mittal.
- Venue: VERIFIED. ICLR 2025 (arXiv comment "Paper accepted to ICLR 2025"; https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/9622163c87b67fd5a4a0ec3247cf356e-Abstract-Conference.html). PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14598 ; https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/9622163c87b67fd5a4a0ec3247cf356e-Paper-Conference.pdf
- Code and data: VERIFIED. https://github.com/SORRY-Bench/sorry-bench (MIT code). Dataset on Hugging Face as sorry-bench-202503 (gated; request access). Judge: fine-tuned Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2. No leaderboard link on the repo; project site sorry-bench.github.io (not opened).
- Size and what it measures: VERIFIED. 44-category taxonomy, 440 class-balanced unsafe instructions (10 per category), 20 linguistic mutations (about 8.8K extra prompts), 7K+ human judgments for judge meta-evaluation, 50+ models. Measures refusal of unsafe requests (and fulfillment rate).
- Citations: 278 (S2, 2026-10-13).
- Use in 2025-2026: no system card, government evaluation or major leaderboard found using it. The repo was last updated March 2025 (per search). A 2026 paper lists it with HarmBench and JBB as safety benchmarks (arXiv 2609.04714, VERIFIED (snippet)). Not in the Inspect Evals listing I read. Overall: academic reuse only, UNVERIFIED for frontier use.
- Criticisms and limitations: authors caution against comparing numbers across benchmarks with different configurations (VERIFIED (snippet)). Gated dataset limits access. Static public set; English-centric apart from mutations. No independent critique found.

## 6. AIR-Bench 2024 (near-miss)

- Exact title: VERIFIED. arXiv: "AIR-Bench 2024: A Safety Benchmark Based on Risk Categories from Regulations and Policies" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.17436). The ICLR 2025 camera-ready title differs: "AIR-BENCH 2024: A Safety Benchmark based on Regulation and Policies Specified Risk Categories" (VERIFIED (snippet): https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/a103529738706979331778377f2d5864-Abstract-Conference.html, listed in search results, page not opened).
- Authors (12, in order): VERIFIED. Yi Zeng, Yu Yang, Andy Zhou, Jeffrey Ziwei Tan, Yuheng Tu, Yifan Mai, Kevin Klyman, Minzhou Pan, Ruoxi Jia, Dawn Song, Percy Liang, Bo Li.
- Venue: ICLR 2025 VERIFIED (snippet) via the ICLR proceedings and iclr.cc/virtual/2025/poster/29470 listings. The arXiv page lists no venue. A NeurIPS 2024 listing also exists (https://neurips.cc/virtual/2024/104206, different title; track unverified, probably a workshop).
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17436 . v1 11 Jul 2024, v2 5 Aug 2024.
- Data and leaderboard: HF stanford-crfm/air-bench-2024 and HELM leaderboard (VERIFIED (snippet) from paper footnotes; the HF page fetch timed out and the HELM page is JS-only). Inspect Evals lists AIR-Bench with 5,694 samples (VERIFIED).
- Size and what it measures: VERIFIED. 5,694 prompts across 314 risk categories (four-tier taxonomy) derived from 8 government regulations and 16 company policies. It measures model refusal/safe handling per regulatory risk category, with an LLM judge.
- Citations: 86 (S2, 2026-10-13).
- Use in 2025-2026:
  - HELM: the HELM Safety v1.0 post says its 24 evaluated models are those on the AIR-Bench leaderboard (VERIFIED, https://crfm.stanford.edu/2024/11/08/helm-safety.html). The paper states it was built on the HELM framework.
  - Inspect Evals includes AIR-Bench (VERIFIED, https://ukgovernmentbeis.github.io/inspect_evals/; directory air_bench seen on GitHub).
  - Follow-up: AIR-BENCH Live (Naphade, Pan, Li; arXiv 2607.22671, 6 Jul 2026), a self-evolving successor (314 to 335 risks; 14 models). VERIFIED. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22671. Its snippet table shows several recent models at or near 1.00 on the 2024 prompts (GPT-5.5 1.00, Claude Opus 4.8 1.00), which suggests ceiling effects, but the abstract does not say 2024 is saturated, and some 1.00 scores reflect API moderation blocking prompts (VERIFIED (snippet)).
  - Frontier system cards, UK AISI/CAISI: not found. UNVERIFIED.
- Criticisms and limitations: authors state it is static and needs periodic updates as regulations change (VERIFIED (snippet)); LLM-judge dependence; the successor paper's premise is that old safety benchmarks go stale. The "Stanford CRFM/Virtue AI" attribution in earlier notes is not supported by the arXiv page (affiliations not shown): UNVERIFIED.
