## 2. AdvBench / Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models (GCG)

- **Exact title**: VERIFIED. "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models". https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043
- **Authors (6)**: VERIFIED. Andy Zou, Zifan Wang, Nicholas Carlini, Milad Nasr, J. Zico Kolter, Matt Fredrikson. A Semantic Scholar BibTeX reportedly omits Carlini and Nasr (search snippet), so use the arXiv list.
- **Venue**: VERIFIED as arXiv-only. The arXiv page shows no journal reference. The comments field says only "Website: llm-attacks.org". v1 was 27 Jul 2023 and v2 was 20 Dec 2023. Semantic Scholar lists it as an arXiv.org preprint (search snippet), and the llm-attacks GitHub README cites only the arXiv ID. A peer-reviewed version: UNVERIFIED. I found none in arXiv, Semantic Scholar or GitHub. The DBLP lookup was blocked (Anubis access-denied), so DBLP is unchecked.
- **Direct PDF**: VERIFIED. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.15043 and https://llm-attacks.org/zou2023universal.pdf
- **Code and data**: VERIFIED. https://github.com/llm-attacks/llm-attacks, MIT licence. The data is in `data/advbench/harmful_behaviors.csv` and `data/advbench/harmful_strings.csv` (raw: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/llm-attacks/llm-attacks/main/data/advbench/harmful_behaviors.csv). No leaderboard for AdvBench itself.
- **Exact counts**:
  - Repository CSVs, which I downloaded and counted on 2026-10-13: Harmful Behaviors has 520 rows (columns `goal`, `target`) and Harmful Strings has 574 rows (column `target`). All rows are distinct as exact strings in both files. VERIFIED by direct count.
  - The paper text itself says "500" strings and "500" harmful behaviors (https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15043). The released files do not match the paper's figure. The 520/574 counts are the ones to cite for the data release.
- **What it measures**: VERIFIED from the paper. Harmful Strings: can the attack make the model emit specific toxic strings (lengths 3 to 44 tokens, mean 16 under the LLaMA tokenizer). Harmful Behaviors: can a single suffix make the model attempt to comply with harmful instructions across many behaviors. HarmBench states that AdvBench ASR "is evaluated using substring matching" (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04249), meaning refusal-string detection, not a harm judge.
- **Known criticisms**:
  - Few distinct behaviors despite 520 rows. HarmBench's comparison table counts 58 unique behaviors in AdvBench, found by "manual and automated semantic deduplication" (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04249). VERIFIED. This is the strongest source.
  - JailbreakBench says "many existing datasets contain duplicated entries, behaviors that are impossible to fulfill, or are not fully open-sourced", naming AdvBench among the stand-alone datasets (https://arxiv.org/html/2404.01318). VERIFIED. This is a general statement about several datasets, not an AdvBench-only finding.
  - Searchability: HarmBench found 50% of 20 sampled AdvBench behaviors were findable by a 10-minute Google search (one author, small sample). VERIFIED, same source.
  - Substring-matching ASR is a weak judge. HarmBench's classifier comparison shows the AdvBench refusal-matching metric at 69.93 average agreement with human labels versus 93.19 for HarmBench's classifier (same source). VERIFIED.
  - A search result claimed that SmoothLLM (Robey et al.) flagged many duplicate AdvBench behaviors. I searched the SmoothLLM text (https://arxiv.org/html/2310.03684) for "duplicate" and found no such statement. This second-hand claim is UNVERIFIED and should not be cited.
  - The earlier notes' "bomb"/explosives-heavy idea has no quantified source. UNVERIFIED.
- **Citation count**: Snapshot only. A search snippet of the Semantic Scholar page shows about 2,230 citations. It is undated, so the live count is UNVERIFIED. The API returned HTTP 429 on every attempt for this paper. Google Scholar: UNVERIFIED.
- **Reuse in 2025-26**:
  - HarmBench says its standard behaviors "are modeled after existing datasets of harmful behaviors, including AdvBench" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04249, arXiv 2024). VERIFIED. HarmBench is 2024, not 2025-26, and its arXiv page lists no venue.
  - JailbreakBench (NeurIPS 2024 D&B) took 18 of its 100 JBB-Behaviors from AdvBench (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01318). VERIFIED. It also built its judge dataset from an AdvBench subset and uses GCG as an attack.
  - 2025-26 papers still use AdvBench per search results, for example https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15427, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02961 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08297. These came from search snippets only and I did not read them. No model card, leaderboard or Inspect Evals use found. The inspect_evals directory listing has no advbench entry (UNVERIFIED beyond that listing).
- **Security venue and blog angle**:
  - The paper did not appear at USENIX Security, S&P, CCS or NDSS as far as I found, and I found no peer-reviewed venue at all. UNVERIFIED as an absence.
  - Carlini and Nasr are co-authors, so Carlini's involvement is authorship, not an independent endorsement. VERIFIED from the arXiv author list.
  - Lilian Weng's Lil'Log post "Adversarial Attacks on LLMs" (https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2023-10-25-adv-attack-llm/) discusses Zou et al. (2023), including GCG and the transfer experiments. VERIFIED by fetching the page.
  - No Anthropic blog highlight found (UNVERIFIED). I found no New York Times article in search results (UNVERIFIED). The earlier notes' statement that AdvBench became "the default jailbreak test set" has support only as widespread reuse (HarmBench and JailbreakBench both drew on it).

## 3. XSTest (near-miss)

- **Exact title**: VERIFIED. "XSTest: A Test Suite for Identifying Exaggerated Safety Behaviours in Large Language Models". https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01263
- **Authors (6)**: VERIFIED. Paul Röttger, Hannah Rose Kirk, Bertie Vidgen, Giuseppe Attanasio, Federico Bianchi, Dirk Hovy. The ACL Anthology lists "Hannah Kirk".
- **Venue**: VERIFIED. NAACL 2024 Main Conference, Long Papers, Mexico City, pages 5377-5400, DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.naacl-long.301. https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.301/
- **PDF**: VERIFIED. https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.301.pdf
- **Data and code**: VERIFIED. https://github.com/paul-rottger/xstest (`xstest_prompts.csv`, CC-BY-4.0 for prompts). Hugging Face copy: https://huggingface.co/datasets/Paul/XSTest. Scripts include `classify_completions.py`.
- **Size and measure**: VERIFIED. 250 safe prompts in ten types that a well-calibrated model should not refuse, plus 200 unsafe contrast prompts it should refuse. It measures exaggerated safety (over-refusal).
- **Citation count**: 590 on Semantic Scholar API, checked 2026-10-13. Google Scholar: UNVERIFIED.
- **Reuse in 2025-26**: VERIFIED for Inspect Evals. The `inspect_evals/xstest` task exists in the UK AISI repo (https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/inspect_evals/tree/main/src/inspect_evals/xstest), and its README uses a `gpt-5-nano` example, so it is current. Claude 3 launch material: a secondary source (https://thezvi.substack.com/p/on-claude-30) quotes Anthropic's XSTest refusal rate falling from 35.1% (Claude 2.1) to 9% (Claude 3). I could not locate the figure in the official model card PDF, so this is UNVERIFIED against the primary source. JailbreakBench added an over-refusal evaluation dataset (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01318), and that dataset's relation to XSTest is not confirmed (UNVERIFIED).
- **Security venue and blog angle**: NLP venue only. No security-venue appearance found. No Carlini, Weng or Anthropic blog highlight verified.
- **Criticisms and limitations**: UNVERIFIED. I did not find a critique. Its size is small (450 prompts), which is a fact about scale, not a published criticism.

## 4. BeaverTails (near-miss)

- **Exact title**: VERIFIED. "BeaverTails: Towards Improved Safety Alignment of LLM via a Human-Preference Dataset". https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.04657
- **Authors**: VERIFIED, with a discrepancy between sources.
  - NeurIPS proceedings list: Jiaming Ji, Mickel Liu, Josef Dai, Xuehai Pan, Chi Zhang, Ce Bian, Boyuan Chen, Ruiyang Sun, Yizhou Wang, Yaodong Yang (https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/4dbb61cb68671edc4ca3712d70083b9f-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html).
  - arXiv abstract page lists "Juntao Dai" instead of "Josef Dai", lists "Chi Zhang" twice and omits "Boyuan Chen".
  - Use the proceedings list.
- **Venue**: VERIFIED. NeurIPS 2023, Datasets and Benchmarks Track (same proceedings page). ACM DL record: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3666122.3667194 (appeared in search results only).
- **PDF**: VERIFIED. https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/file/4dbb61cb68671edc4ca3712d70083b9f-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf. The arXiv PDF is https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04657.
- **Data and code**: VERIFIED. https://huggingface.co/datasets/PKU-Alignment/BeaverTails (CC BY-NC 4.0, 14 harm categories, a QA pair can carry several). Splits: 330k_train 301k rows, 330k_test 33.4k, 30k_train 27.2k, 30k_test 3.02k. Also https://github.com/PKU-Alignment/beavertails and a small evaluation set at https://huggingface.co/datasets/PKU-Alignment/BeaverTails-Evaluation (under 1K prompts, per a search snippet). The dataset card says it should not be used to train dialogue agents.
- **Size and measure**: VERIFIED from the arXiv abstract. Safety meta-labels for 333,963 QA pairs and 361,903 expert comparison pairs, with helpfulness and harmlessness annotated separately. The 30k release has 30,207 QA pairs (search snippet, UNVERIFIED against primary).
- **Citation count**: Snapshot only. A search snippet of the Semantic Scholar page shows 692, undated and reportedly cached. Live count UNVERIFIED (API rate-limited). OpenAlex shows 69 for the proceedings record and is not comparable.
- **Reuse in 2025-26**: PARTLY VERIFIED. Promptfoo has a BeaverTails red-team plugin (https://www.promptfoo.dev/docs/red-team/plugins/beavertails) and DeepTeam documents a BeaverTails framework page (https://trydeepteam.com/docs/frameworks-beavertails). These came from search results and I did not read them. Not found in the inspect_evals directory listing. Research reuse (for example https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.00166, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.22557) is from search snippets only.
- **Security venue and blog angle**: ML venue only. No security-venue appearance found. No Carlini, Weng or Anthropic highlight verified.
- **Criticisms and limitations**: UNVERIFIED as published critiques. Documented on the dataset card: non-commercial licence (CC BY-NC 4.0) and a warning against training dialogue agents on it.
