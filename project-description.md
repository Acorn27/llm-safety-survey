# CSE 6324: Project

## 1. Overview

In this project, you will conduct a literature survey on a topic of your choice related to **Trustworthy AI**.

The goal is not simply to collect and summarize papers. Instead, you should review and synthesize the literature by identifying:

- the major research problems;
- the major technical approaches;
- how the different approaches are evaluated;
- the strengths and limitations of existing approaches;
- important open research questions; and
- representative research papers, software tools, datasets, benchmarks, and other resources.

Through the process, you should develop a strong understanding of the topic and the current state of research on the topic. 

You will produce two deliverables:

1. A resource website that collects and organizes the literature and technical resources related to your topic.
2. A 25-minute class presentation that synthesizes the literature and presents the current state of research on the topic.

The website should be useful to someone who wants to quickly learn about the topic and locate important papers, tools, datasets, benchmarks, and other resources.

You may complete this project individually or in a two-person team. If you work in a two-person team, identify both team members and notify the TA by **Oct. 15** by email. Both team members are expected to contribute substantially to the project and participate equally in the presentation.

## 2. Topic Selection

Select one topic related to **Trustworthy AI**. The following research areas and topics are provided as examples, but you may also propose your own topic. If you decide to propose your own topic please remember it must be approved by the Instructor. You should finalize the topic by **Oct. 22**:

- **Explainable AI:** Mechanistic interpretations; explanation methods for deep neural networks; explanation faithfulness and stability.

- **AI Testing and Debugging:** Test input generation; failure analysis and failure region discovery; fault localization and root cause analysis; regression testing and testing of model updates; testing of LLMs and LLM-based systems.

- **AI Security:** Adversarial attacks and defenses; backdoor attacks and defenses; data poisoning attacks and defenses; model extraction attacks and defenses.

- **LLM Safety and Security:** Jailbreak attacks and defenses; prompt injection attacks and defenses; red teaming; safety evaluation and benchmarking; safety alignment methods and their evaluation.

- **AI Fairness:** Fairness testing; fairness and bias in LLMs; fairness-aware model training; fairness mitigation and repair.

- **AI Privacy:** Membership inference attacks and defenses; training data extraction and privacy leakage in LLMs; differential privacy for machine learning; machine unlearning; privacy auditing and leakage assessment; privacy in federated learning.

- **AI Agent Security:** Tool use security; poisoning attacks against agent memory and knowledge bases; security of multi-agent communication; red teaming and security evaluation of AI agents.

- **AI Agent Safety:** Agent failure detection and recovery; agent self-correction and reflection; runtime monitoring and guardrails; safety evaluation and benchmarking; fail-safe, intervention, and recovery mechanisms.

You should select a topic with sufficient research depth to support a meaningful literature survey. As a general guideline, you should be able to identify at least 10 high-quality research papers, preferably 15–20, that are directly relevant to your topic. You may need to explore several candidate topics before finalizing your selection. If a topic is too broad, narrow its scope. If it has too little existing research, broaden the scope or combine it with a closely related topic.

## 3. Literature Survey

Identify approximately 10–20 high-quality research papers that collectively provide a meaningful survey of the selected topic. Your paper selection should include, where appropriate:

- foundational or influential work;
- important papers representing the major technical approaches; and
- recent work reflecting the current research frontier.

Prioritize peer-reviewed papers from established conferences and journals. Recent preprints may also be included when they are particularly relevant.

Whenever possible, develop a conceptual framework to organize the literature into meaningful categories. The survey should emphasize synthesis and comparison across papers, rather than a collection of independent paper summaries.

For each major approach or category, consider questions such as:

- What problem does it address?
- What is the central idea and the key insight behind it?
- What assumptions does it make?
- How is it evaluated?
- What are its strengths and limitations?
- How does it differ from competing approaches?
- What important problems remain unresolved?

## 4. Website

Create a publicly accessible website using GitHub, Google Sites, or another platform. The website does not need a sophisticated design, but it should have a clean and professional look and feel. Evaluation will focus primarily on the technical content, organization, and usefulness of the site.

The website should include the following sections:

### 4.1 Topic Overview

Provide a concise introduction that explains:

- the topic and why it matters to trustworthy AI;
- the scope of your survey; and
- how the resources on the website are organized.

### 4.2 Research Papers

For each selected paper, provide:

- title;
- authors;
- venue and year; and
- a link to the paper, preferably to a publicly available PDF when possible.

For each paper, provide a short description of its main contributions, typically in no more than five sentences.

### 4.3 Software and Other Resources

Identify relevant technical resources, where available, such as:

- software and libraries;
- reference implementations;
- datasets;
- benchmarks; and
- evaluation frameworks.

For each selected resource, provide a short description of its key features, typically in no more than five sentences.

## 5. Presentation

Prepare a 25-minute presentation that teaches the class about your topic. The presentation should synthesize the field, rather than summarize papers one by one.

A recommended structure is:

- introduction, motivation, and scope;
- major research problems;
- major technical approaches;
- comparison of representative approaches;
- critical assessment of their strengths and limitations; and
- open problems and research opportunities.

You should be prepared to explain the technical ideas behind the major approaches. Clearly distinguish claims made by prior work from your own interpretation.
