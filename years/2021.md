## Conference findings
- ACL/NAACL 2021: BAD (NAACL) and DynaHate (ACL) are the clearest safety-dataset papers, both on adversarial, human-in-the-loop data collection.
- ICLR 2021: ETHICS. NeurIPS 2021: Jiminy Cricket.
- USENIX Security 2021: Carlini et al. extraction attack (venue unverified).
- Awards: not checked; do not assume any.
- Not searched: IEEE S&P, CCS, NDSS, EMNLP 2021, ICML 2021 for additional safety benchmarks. Gap in coverage.

## Blog/researcher signals
- Hendrycks, Carlini, Schulman, Steinhardt, "Unsolved Problems in ML Safety" (arXiv 2109.13916, Sep 2021): agenda paper that frames robustness, monitoring, alignment, and systemic safety; a roadmap rather than a benchmark.
- Askell et al. (Anthropic), "A General Language Assistant as a Laboratory for Alignment" (arXiv 2112.00861, Dec 2021): introduces HHH framing and alignment evaluations; reports benefits of prompting and preference modeling scale with model size; v3 corrected the TruthfulQA metric.
- Weidinger et al. (DeepMind), "Ethical and social risks of harm from Language Models" (arXiv 2112.04359, Dec 2021): taxonomy of risks; agenda for evaluation, not a dataset.
- Gopher paper (Rae et al., arXiv 2112.11446, Dec 2021): abstract confirms toxicity and bias analysis; whether it used TruthfulQA/RealToxicityPrompts was not confirmed from the page.
- Not verified: OpenAI blog "PALMS" (June 2021), CRFM "On the Opportunities and Risks of Foundation Models" (Aug 2021) are known from memory only.

## News/product adoption
- Chen et al., "Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code" (Codex, arXiv 2107.03374, Jul 2021) includes a discussion of safety, security, and economic implications (abstract-verified). Not a benchmark, but an early system-level risk analysis.
- No evidence gathered of government evals, regulation, or system cards using these benchmarks in 2021. System cards did not yet exist as a norm (from memory).

## State of development
In 2021 LLM safety evaluation was nascent and fragmented. Toxicity and hate speech dominated (BAD, DynaHate, with RealToxicityPrompts from 2020 as the background standard), with an emerging moral/value strand (ETHICS, Jiminy Cricket) and one truthfulness benchmark (TruthfulQA) that would turn out to be the most durable. Methodologically, adversarial human-and-model-in-the-loop data collection appeared as a technique.

Risk frameworks and agendas (Unsolved Problems in ML Safety, DeepMind's risk taxonomy, Anthropic's HHH paper) outnumbered concrete benchmarks, and evaluations for jailbreaks, dangerous capabilities, and agents were essentially absent.

## Gaps / areas for improvement
- No jailbreak or misuse/dangerous-capability benchmarks; no instruction-following or chat-model safety suites.
- Toxicity and bias evals relied on classifiers (e.g., Perspective-style), which are noisy.
- Small, static, English-only datasets.
- Truthfulness evaluated by multiple-choice proxies or human judgment; scalability limited.
- Little standardization or reporting practice.

## Future directions
- TruthfulQA became a standard line item in 2022+ model reports.
- Anthropic's HHH/preference work and the BAD-style red teaming pointed to red-team datasets and harmlessness evals (2022).
- Jiminy Cricket and ETHICS pointed toward agent-safety and machine-ethics evals.
- Extraction attacks set up privacy/memorization evaluation.
