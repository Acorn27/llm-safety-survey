## Conference findings
- ICLR 2026: 5,355 accepted / 8,408 rejected (27.4%). SafeDialBench appears as an ICLR 2026 paper; RedBench was a workshop paper. No official accepted list was checked.
- ACL 2026: RiskAtlas reported accepted (main), from a lab page only.
- USENIX Security 2026: only found a non-benchmark paper; no LLM safety benchmark verified.
- NeurIPS 2026 D&B, CCS, EMNLP 2026, S&P, NDSS: not searched successfully; no awards found. Gap in this report.
- Trend visible in arXiv: shift from text jailbreak sets to agent/OS-grounded and judge-reliability benchmarks.

## Blog/researcher signals
- International AI Safety Report 2026 (Feb 2026) stresses an "evaluation gap": benchmarks poorly predict real-world risk, contamination/saturation, and evaluation-aware model behavior (the last point is secondhand from explainers). Its highlighted benchmarks are mostly capability ones (SWE-bench Verified, GPQA, FrontierMath).
- NIST CAISI: AI Agent Standards Initiative launched 17 Feb 2026; agent hijacking evals built on AgentDojo; lessons: adaptive attacks, task-level results, multiple attempts (via CSA notes and NIST blog). Joint red-teaming competition with Gray Swan and UK AISI (13 models, >250k attacks, every model hijacked at least once; secondary source).
- Not covered: Lilian Weng, Carlini, CRFM, CAIS posts.

## News/product adoption
- OpenAI GPT-5.6 and GPT-6 Astra system cards: prompt-injection evals including Gray Swan (1,810 curated attacks), automated red-teamer "GPT-Red", agentic misalignment evals (card pages found; contents via search summary).
- Anthropic Claude Sonnet 5 system card (30 Jun 2026): malicious-use of coding/computer-use agents, influence ops, prompt injection incl. a new benchmark. Opus 4.8 card (28 May 2026) per VentureBeat; its numbers (31.5% browser agent hijack) are unverified secondary.
- VentureBeat: Google moved this disclosure to a separate framework; Meta no closed-model card.
- Regulation/EU AI Act linkage not researched.

## State of development
Lab system cards in 2026 lean on in-house and vendor-partner (Gray Swan) agentic prompt-injection and misuse evaluations rather than classic public datasets, while academic work produces many new agent-focused benchmarks (LITMUS, ClawSafety, NRT-Bench) that test real actions in sandboxes. MLCommons continues the standardized, graded approach (AILuminate to Jailbreak benchmark), and government (CAISI, UK AISI) emphasizes agent hijacking via AgentDojo.

A second theme is measurement validity: catalogues and judge-reliability papers report fragmentation, inconsistent LLM judges, and weak benchmark governance. No 2026 benchmark has clearly achieved broad adoption yet; most are months old.

## Gaps / areas for improvement
- Safety-judge unreliability (MLCommons reports ~16% misses, ~38% false flags, via secondary source).
- Verbal refusal versus actual action mismatch; text-only grading misses it.
- Contamination/saturation and evaluation-awareness of models.
- Fragmentation and poor governance of benchmarks; little low-resource-language coverage (Chinese judge benchmark is a start).
- Venue verification here is incomplete.

## Future directions
Sandboxed, state-verified agent evaluations; adaptive/automated red-teaming as standard; renewable benchmarks to resist contamination; validated judges with error margins; standardization through CAISI/MLCommons.
