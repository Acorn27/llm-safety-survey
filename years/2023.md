## Conference findings
- NeurIPS 2023 D&B: DecodingTrust won an Outstanding Paper award, confirmed by arXiv and UChicago news. BeaverTails was also published at NeurIPS 2023.
- NeurIPS 2023 main: Carlini et al. (adversarial alignment); Jailbroken (my recollection).
- ICML 2023: MACHIAVELLI (oral).
- NAACL 2024 and ACL 2024 accepted XSTest and SafetyBench, which were posted in 2023.
- I did not systematically search USENIX Security, S&P, CCS, NDSS, ICLR, ACL or EMNLP 2023 proceedings, so coverage of those venues is incomplete. The DAN paper is a security-community paper, but I could not verify its venue.

## Blog/researcher signals
- Google DeepMind's "Model evaluation for extreme risks" (May 2023) set the dangerous-capability vs. alignment evaluation distinction.
- Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy v1.0 was effective 19 Sep 2023 (per Anthropic's RSP page). A secondary summary says it ties capability evaluations to safety levels (ASL), which I treat cautiously.
- I did not find a Lilian Weng, CAIS, CRFM or NIST post for 2023 in my searches. I make no claims about them.

## News/product adoption
- Llama 2 (Jul 2023) reported TruthfulQA, ToxiGen and BOLD plus human safety evaluations (arXiv abstract plus a later paper citing it). This is the main case of classic benchmarks reaching a flagship release.
- The GPT-4 Technical Report/system card includes safety work. I could not read its details, so I cannot say which benchmarks it used.
- Meta's Purple Llama (Dec 2023) bundled CyberSecEval and Llama Guard.
- Policy: US Executive Order 14110 (30 Oct 2023) directed NIST to produce red-teaming guidelines and required safety test result reporting for dual-use foundation models. The Bletchley Summit (1-2 Nov 2023) produced a declaration signed by 28 countries plus the EU, and the UK AI Safety Institute was announced there. Neither named specific benchmarks in the sources I read.

## State of development
2023 is the year LLM safety evaluation became its own field after ChatGPT. Evaluation moved from toxicity-only tests (RealToxicityPrompts, ToxiGen) toward multi-axis suites (DecodingTrust, SafetyBench), refusal and over-refusal test sets (Do-Not-Answer, XSTest, SimpleSafetyTests), adversarial jailbreaking (AdvBench/GCG, the DAN study, Jailbroken), and early agent and cyber evaluations (MACHIAVELLI, ToolEmu, CyberSecEval). Many of these were small test suites of a few hundred prompts, often scored by GPT-4 judges.

At the policy level, evaluation of "extreme risks" became a governance concept (DeepMind framework, Anthropic RSP, US EO, Bletchley), but concrete, standardized benchmarks for catastrophic capabilities did not yet exist. They arrived in 2024 (e.g., WMDP, which I only saw as an arXiv ID).

## Gaps / areas for improvement
- No standard attack or judge protocol. AdvBench is small and repetitive, and results across papers are not comparable (HarmBench in 2024 addressed this).
- Reliance on GPT-4 as judge; static, English-heavy datasets (SafetyBench is bilingual).
- Little coverage of multi-turn, multimodal, agentic and catastrophic-misuse evaluation.
- Many key papers were preprints at the time.

## Future directions
- Standardized red-teaming frameworks (HarmBench, 2402.04249).
- Hazardous-knowledge benchmarks such as WMDP (2403.03218).
- Broader trust suites (TrustLLM) and agent-safety benchmarks.
- Frontier-lab and AISI pre-deployment evaluations built on the 2023 policy commitments.
