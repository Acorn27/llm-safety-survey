## State of development
2024 was the year safety evaluation standardized. The field moved from ad hoc harmful-prompt lists to reproducible frameworks with fixed threat models, judges and leaderboards (HarmBench, JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT), showing that attack-success numbers were inconsistent and often inflated. Parallel tracks emerged: dangerous-capability proxies with unlearning (WMDP), over-refusal (XSTest, SORRY-Bench), policy-grounded taxonomies (AIR-Bench, MLCommons, SALAD), and open safety classifiers/judges (WildGuard, MD-Judge).

A second shift was toward agents: AgentHarm and AgentDojo extended evaluation from single-turn text to tool use and prompt injection. Government institutes (UK/US AISI) began pre-deployment testing, and labs began citing academic jailbreak benchmarks in system cards.

## Gaps / areas for improvement
- LLM-judge reliability and inconsistent harm definitions across benchmarks.
- English-centric, single-turn prompts; limited multilingual and multi-turn coverage (SORRY-Bench starts on linguistic mutations).
- Static test sets prone to contamination and saturation.
- WMDP is MCQ knowledge proxy, not uplift; weak link to real-world risk.
- Over-refusal vs. safety trade-off only partially measured.
- Multimodal safety barely covered here.

## Future directions
Agent safety (AgentHarm, AgentDojo lineage), standards-body benchmarks (MLCommons v0.5 to v1.0), regulation-grounded taxonomies (AIR-Bench), government and third-party pre-deployment evals (AISI Inspect), reasoning-model jailbreak robustness (o1), and judge-quality research.
